# Dil De Baithi: Tilt-Shift Miniature lyric film (treatment)

**Song:** *Dil De Baithi*, Dhanda Nyoliwala (4:02, original audio, ~130 BPM).
**Style:** Tilt-Shift Miniature (lemo-opuscar `styles/tilt-shift`): a real-looking Haryana village seen from high above through a tilted lens, so it reads as a tabletop model.
**Lyrics:** word timings from the v2 alignment (Whisper + isolated vocals), `tools/hyperframes/dil-de-baithi/lyrics.json`.

## The place
A small Haryana village as a freshly painted model. It has:
- havelis with flat rooftops, parapets, water tanks, clotheslines and kites;
- a lane with a tractor, a bullock cart and a Bullet motorbike;
- a mustard field in saturated yellow, a village pond (johad) with buffaloes, and a big peepal tree on a round chabutra;
- a small white temple with a saffron flag, and a tubewell.

Palette: whitewash and sandstone walls, candy accents (pink, turquoise and saffron doors), saturated green and mustard-yellow fields, blue-clean shadows. Night is low-saturation, with warm windows.

## The tiny actors
- **Her:** red-pink dupatta, lives in the haveli with the turquoise door.
- **Him:** white kurta, on a black Bullet.

They are the only two saturated things that move against the village's flow. The sharp band always finds them.

## Three candidate structures
1. **One day, dawn to dawn.** A single locked vantage; the sun, shadows and lights sweep while the couple's story plays in the band. Clear, but 4 minutes from one angle is too static for a music video.
2. **The relay.** The band hands attention from villager to villager until it reaches the couple. Charming, but it delays the lovers, and the song is about them from the first chorus.
3. **Two rooftops, one village day-and-night (chosen).** The story runs between her rooftop and his lane:
   - choruses are wide, busy, noon time-lapse views of the whole village;
   - verses dive closer to the pair;
   - the clock runs noon → dusk → night (stars, first meeting) → rain → day → winter fog → dawn at the temple.

   This gives a natural lighting arc, variety of camera grammar, and the style's native moves land on the song's key moments.
